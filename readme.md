# DOA Estimation with DA-MUSIC: Data-Driven Direction of Arrival Estimation via Deep Augmented MUSIC Algorithm [with Python Code]

Multi-signal Direction of Arrival (DoA) estimation is crucial in sensor array signal processing. A common method for multi-signal DoA estimation is the Multiple Signal Classification (MUSIC) algorithm, which achieves high-performance super-resolution DoA recovery and is widely applied in practice. MUSIC is a model-based algorithm that relies on the precise mathematical description of the relationship between signals and measurements and the assumptions about the signals themselves (non-coherent, narrowband), making it sensitive to model flaws. In this study, we enhance this algorithm by using a specially designed neural architecture to overcome these limitations of MUSIC. Our proposed Deep Augmented MUSIC (DA-MUSIC) algorithm is a hybrid DoA estimator based on both model and data-driven approaches, leveraging data to improve performance and robustness while preserving the interpretable process of classical methods. DA-MUSIC can overcome the limitations of purely model-based methods, such as successfully localizing coherent sources and estimating the number of coherent signal sources. We further demonstrate the outstanding resolution of DA-MUSIC algorithms in synthetic narrow-band and wide-band scenarios as well as real seismic signal DoA estimation.
Introduction: Signal source separation, localization, and tracking are key tasks in sensor array processing. Particularly, the direction of arrival (DoA) estimation of multiple, broadband, and possibly coherent signal sources plays a critical role in numerous applications such as radar, communication, image analysis, and speech enhancement. Over the past few decades, a large number of different DoA estimation algorithms have been proposed, and the field is still actively researched.
In many DoA applications, the Multiple Signal Classification (MUSIC) algorithm is a mainstream solution that can asymptotically unbiasedly estimate the number of incident wavefronts, their approximate frequencies, and DoA. However, MUSIC and other classical methods (such as traditional beamforming and MVDR beamforming) rely on an understanding of underlying statistical models, which introduces certain limitations. One of the drawbacks of model-based (MB) approaches is that the signal models they are based on have inherent limitations, focusing only on narrowband signals. This leads to inconsistent estimation of DoA for correlated signals and difficulties in distinguishing closely spaced signals when the sample size is insufficient or the SNR is low.
To extend narrowband models to broadband DoA estimation, various extensions and alternatives have been explored. Since when the incident signal is wideband, multiple frequency bands can carry different information related to DoA angles. Generally speaking, methods from narrowband to broadband can be categorized into coherent processing and non-coherent processing. In coherent processing, the observation covariance matrices across different frequency bins are combined through specific transformation matrices. Most coherent techniques are based on the concept of Coherent Signal Subspace (CSS), which transforms the covariance across different frequencies into a substitute narrowband model but using different transformation matrices. Non-coherent broadband processing involves combining the DoA estimates obtained from each frequency bin separately.
In recent years, data-driven (DD) and deep learning (DL) have achieved success in various fields, prompting the emergence of DoA estimators assisted by neural networks (NN). Some studies have independently implemented DoA estimation using dense neural networks, convolutional neural networks (CNN), sparsely connected CNNs, and U-Net architectures. Other methods leverage knowledge from spatial covariance matrices, some training CNNs based on classification tasks to also handle unknown number of signal sources, while others analyze pure data-driven estimators and combine data-driven approaches with maximum likelihood estimation (MLE). Additionally, there are studies that combine DL with MLE, employing multiple dense neural networks and ResNets to estimate subsets of candidate angles, and research expanding this architecture to use ResNets to estimate the number of signal sources. Although these black-box neural networks can handle array artifacts due to their model-agnostic nature, they involve highly parameterized models, high computational costs, and lack interpretability based on models (MB) as compared to other methods.
Additionally, neural networks have been employed to enhance model-based MUSIC, forming a hybrid MB/DD system. Specifically, research has proposed estimating the discrete MUSIC spectrum from the measured (spatial smoothing) covariance matrix using multiple convolutional neural networks. Although these methods are more robust than the original MUSIC algorithm in terms of model accuracy, using MUSIC spectrum as training labels poses the same disadvantages as those found in model-based approaches. Another data-driven method considers systems with subarray sampling and uses neural networks to obtain individual estimation covariance matrices from uncorrelated subarray measurements, followed by DoA recovery via a subspace-based MUSIC algorithm. This approach addresses the fundamental dependence of MUSIC on the estimated covariance matrix, thereby enhancing the robustness of the MUSIC algorithm. However, it does not fully leverage the capabilities of neural networks to improve MUSIC, as the neural network is trained using real covariance matrices as labels.

Conclusion This paper proposes a hybrid MUSIC algorithm implementation based on model and data driven (DA-MUSIC), for direction of arrival (DoA) estimation. This method can alleviate some limitations of the classical MUSIC algorithm and can effectively work in scenarios where the number of signal sources is unknown or when processing wideband signals, and it exhibits strong robustness against severe mismatches in array geometry. This hybrid approach provides viable alternatives in both low and high snapshot scenarios, showing significant resolution advantages compared to both model-based and purely data-driven baseline methods in various scenarios.
